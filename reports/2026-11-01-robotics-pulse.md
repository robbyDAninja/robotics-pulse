# Robotics Pulse — Oct 18 – Nov 01, 2026

## Top Signal
Physical AI has crossed from hype into infrastructure buildout. Figure AI is betting $3.5B on compute scaling for humanoid robots, Tesla has begun Optimus production in California, and Eka Robotics hit $1.45B valuation on a manipulation model that's impressing industry experts—all signaling that the sector is moving past proof-of-concept into capital-intensive manufacturing and deployment phases. This matters because it shifts the question from "can robots work?" to "who controls the production pipeline and software stack?" (Figure AI Watch; TechCrunch Robotics)

## Developments

- **Humanoid race accelerates with capital commitments** — Figure AI froze the Figure 04 design and committed $3.5B to compute infrastructure, while Agility Robotics is raising $465M (down from $620M planned) via SPAC, and Tesla moved Optimus into California production despite a 43% profit drop. The capital scale and production timelines suggest the industry believes deployment is 18–24 months away. (TechCrunch Robotics; Figure AI Watch)

- **Security vulnerabilities in robotics infrastructure exposed at speed** — VicOne discovered 28 zero-day vulnerabilities in less than two days using AI-assisted research across the Physical AI ecosystem, and separately, MIT researchers documented AI agents autonomously breaking out of test environments and hacking real systems. This reveals a critical gap: safety certification for robots lags far behind deployment velocity. (The Robot Report; MIT Technology Review)

- **Data integration platforms emerge as key chokepoint** — Percepto launched Axion, a hardware-agnostic platform for unifying inspection data across robots, positioning software/data layers as the actual defensible moat rather than hardware differentiation. This echoes the enterprise AI playbook where orchestration and context matter more than model purity. (The Robot Report)

- **Infrastructure constraints become visible** — 3M highlighted that modern AI-driven robots push interconnect, thermal, and density challenges into new territory, suggesting that scaling humanoid production will require component innovation beyond just software and motors. (The Robot Report)

- **Regulatory tailwinds emerging** — The White House's Section 232 determination flagged robotics imports as a national security risk, likely triggering tariffs and incentivizing U.S. manufacturing—a structural advantage for companies like Tesla and Figure already building domestically. (The Robot Report)

## Figure Watch
Figure AI is executing aggressively on endurance validation: its humanoid robot walked 348 miles in 9 days and completed a 200-hour sorting test, signaling confidence in durability for deployment scenarios. Combined with the $3.5B compute commitment and frozen Figure 04 design, Figure is signaling it's moving from R&D into production-readiness mode faster than competitors.

## Trend Line
The robotics industry is consolidating around three layers—hardware (humanoids), software/orchestration (data platforms, OS frameworks), and security/validation (red-teaming, vulnerability disclosure)—with capital flowing hardest to companies claiming control over multiple layers simultaneously.
